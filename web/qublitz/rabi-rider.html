🌀 Rabi Rider a Bloch-sphere flying game

Your qubit flies across a map of the Bloch sphere: longitude φ runs left to right, and the polar angle runs from |0⟩ at the top to |1⟩ at the bottom. The detuning keeps it precessing about z, which is the scrolling. You steer with the drive, rotating the state about x, y and z, to pass through rings (target states) and dodge noise. Part of Qublitz, where the same rotations show up as Rabi and Ramsey experiments.

The physics in the game

In the frame rotating with the drive, a qubit driven at Rabi rate Ω with phase α and detuned by Δ has

H = (ħ/2)[Δ σz + Ω (cos α σx + sin α σy)],  dr/dt = ω × r,  ω = (Ω cos α, Ω sin α, Δ)

so the Bloch vector r precesses about ω. The Δ part turns it about z at a steady rate: that is why the map scrolls. A drive about an axis in the equator changes the polar angle at

dθ/dt = −Ω sin(φ − α)

which depends on where you are. The same +X drive moves you up on one side of the sphere and down on the other:

Autopilot locks the drive phase to α = φ ± 90°, the axis perpendicular to your meridian, so the drive only changes θ. Holding uses the phase that climbs, letting go adds π. Pilot mode gives you the four equatorial axes, ±X and ±Y, and leaves the choice to you. The drive amplitude ramps with a 0.16 s time constant, like a real bandwidth-limited pulse.

Rings are target pure states. You collect one when the state fidelity |⟨target|ψ⟩|² = (1 + r·t)/2 is high enough, about 0.99; a dead-center pass scores a perfect. Named rings sit at their real places on the sphere: |+⟩ at +x, |+i⟩ at +y, |−⟩ at −x, |−i⟩ at −y, and |1⟩ along the whole bottom edge, because a pole is a single point that the map stretches into a line. The same stretching makes pillars flare out near the poles: every meridian meets every other one there, so a pillar that reaches a pole covers it entirely.

T₁ decay is modeled the way a continuously watched qubit behaves. While no photon is detected, the state stays pure and slides toward |0⟩ at dθ/dt = −(γ/2) sin θ. A photon is detected with probability γ P(|1⟩) per unit time, and the state jumps to |0⟩. Averaged over many runs this is ordinary exponential decay, e−t/T₁. In game time T₁ = 14 s; on a real transmon it is closer to 100 µs.

Quiz gates are two purple rings at the same longitude, so they differ only in θ, and the question picks one of them by its measurement statistics. The Born rule gives P(|1⟩) = sin²(θ/2) and P(|0⟩) = cos²(θ/2), so ⟨σz⟩ = cos θ. Probability is not proportional to height: P(|1⟩) = ¼ sits at θ = 60°, not 45°. A pulse of area A tilts the Bloch vector by A, while the angle that appears in the ket cos(θ/2)|0⟩ + eiφ sin(θ/2)|1⟩ is half of it, which is the trap in the pulse questions.

Power-ups are named after real tools. π, a spin echo: a π pulse in the middle of a sequence flips the qubit so that slow noise cancels itself; in the game it absorbs one hit. Δ↓, recalibration: moving the drive closer to the qubit frequency shrinks the detuning Δ, so the precession (the scrolling) slows for 6 s. T₁↑, a Purcell filter: it keeps the qubit from leaking energy into its readout line, so there are no quantum jumps for 10 s.

Gate tokens apply a single-qubit gate at once, as a fast calibrated pulse would. Their axis follows your longitude, so only θ changes and you stay on your meridian. X is a π rotation about the axis pointing at your longitude, (cos φ, sin φ, 0), which reflects the state through the equator: θ → 180° − θ, so |0⟩ ↔ |1⟩. √X is a π/2 rotation about the axis across your meridian, which turns the state 90° toward the other pole.

Noise (the clouds and pillars) costs a heart of coherence and relaxes the qubit to |0⟩, the ground state. Measurement gates, the blue curtains, are projective measurements of σz: crossing one sends the state to |1⟩ with probability sin²(θ/2) and to |0⟩ otherwise, and it stays there. Each gate pays for one outcome, so the way to win is to load the dice by flying close to that pole first.

The 3D view puts the camera just behind the qubit, outside the sphere and level with the equator, looking along the direction of precession with |0⟩ up. The sphere turning under you is the detuning Δ at work. Everything you fly through hovers at the qubit's flying height; whether you hit it is still decided by angles on the sphere. Press V for the flat map, where longitude runs across the screen.

Missions are short scripted courses, one idea each: Rabi rotations, the π pulse, the equator and its named states, the Born rule, measurement, how the drive axis matters, noise, the lab tricks above, gate shortcuts, quantum jumps, and a final exam.
