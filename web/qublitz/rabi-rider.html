🌀 Rabi Rider ride a Rabi oscillation

Your qubit's height is its polar angle θ on the Bloch sphere, drawn with the ground state |0⟩ at the bottom and the excited state |1⟩ at the top, so height is energy, with P(1) = sin²(θ/2) in between. The course scrolls past in time. The drive is exactly on resonance, so nothing precesses: hold ▲ to turn the qubit toward |1⟩ and ▼ toward |0⟩. Let go and only T₁ decay acts, and it always pulls the qubit down to |0⟩, like gravity. You fly an ensemble of 240 runs of the same experiment, all driven by the same pulse; each emits its photon and drops to |0⟩ at its own random moment, and the faint ghosts show 12 of them. The big marker is their mean, the density matrix; aim it through the rings, because the share of runs that succeed is the ensemble's fidelity with the ring's state. Decay spreads the runs and makes that harder; a green reset starts a fresh shot, and cyan T₁+ chips are real fixes that make the qubit decay more slowly, while red T₁− chips, real faults, make it decay faster. A shorter T₁ is a harder game. For rotations about x, y and z in 3D, try 🚁 Qubit Copter; to run the same pulses as an experiment, open the Virtual Qubit Lab.

The physics

In the frame rotating with a drive that is exactly on resonance there is no free precession. A drive of phase 0 (+X) or π (−X) gives

H = ±(ħΩ/2) σx,  dr/dt = ω × r,  ω = (±Ω, 0, 0),  P(1) = sin²(θ/2)

so the Bloch vector turns about the x axis at the Rabi rate Ω (here 1.6 rad/s: a π pulse, |0⟩ → |1⟩, takes about 2 s; on a transmon it typically takes 10–50 nanoseconds). A turn about x keeps the state on the circle through |0⟩ and |1⟩ in the y–z plane, so its height θ says everything, which is why the game can draw it as a line. It is drawn with |0⟩ at the bottom (the usual Bloch sphere puts it at the top; the sphere on the right is the usual one turned upside down about x), so height is energy and decay falls. ▲ and ▼ pick the drive's sign that turns θ the way you ask, and the drive eases off as the state reaches |0⟩ or |1⟩. A constant drive would carry the state through |1⟩ and back again, P(1) = sin²(Ωt/2), a Rabi oscillation; the game stops it at the poles and reverses the drive when you ask. The phase in the ket, cos(θ/2)|0⟩ ∓ i sin(θ/2)|1⟩, is the −i that a turn about x leaves; it does not change P(1), but a turn about y or z would feel it (see Qubit Copter).

Measurement gates are projective measurements of σz: the state jumps to |1⟩ with probability sin²(θ/2) and to |0⟩ otherwise (the Born rule). X tokens apply the X gate, a π turn about x, so θ → 180° − θ. Noise clouds are stray coupling to the environment, which acts as a measurement nobody asked for: touching one collapses the state to |1⟩ with probability sin²(θ/2) and to |0⟩ otherwise, exactly like a measurement gate. That is decoherence: the environment learns the state, and the superposition is gone. Decoherence here is T₁ alone, energy loss, always on as for a real qubit. Each run follows it the way a photon counter would see it: at the rate P(1)/T₁ the run emits a photon (the yellow wave) and is left in |0⟩, a quantum jump, most often from near |1⟩. Between photons a superposition slides slowly down toward |0⟩, dθ/dt = −sin θ/(2T₁): watching and seeing no photon is itself evidence that the run is lower. |1⟩ itself does not slide; it waits, then jumps. Averaged over runs this is exactly the T₁ master equation: P(1) ∝ e−t/T₁, and the sideways part of the mean (the coherence) fades half as fast, as e−t/2T₁. The difficulty is T₁: 12, 6, 3 or 1.5 s (4 s in One qubit, one photon, 5 s in Jumps in the crowd, 2.5 s in Build a better qubit), only a few π pulses long so you can see it happen; a transmon's T₁ of about 100 µs lasts thousands of 20 ns π pulses. Keeping a qubit excited means fighting decay, which is why fast gates matter.

T₁+ chips each make T₁ 35 % longer, up to four times where it started, and each is named for a real fix: a Purcell filter on the readout line, infrared and magnetic shielding, cleaner surfaces, tantalum films and better packaging. Each closes one place the qubit's energy can leak to, and together, over 25 years, fixes like these took transmon T₁ from nanoseconds to hundreds of microseconds. The game's 35 % per chip is for play; the real gains came one paper at a time. Red T₁− chips are the other direction, real ways T₁ gets worse: a defect drifting into resonance, a light leak, a trapped vortex, a lossy seam in the box. Each takes T₁ down by the same factor, to no less than half where it started; dodge them.

The ghosts. An experiment is many runs of the same pulse sequence, and its result is an average over them. The game flies 240 runs at once with one drive and draws a few of them. Each run is a pure state that jumps on its own (their photon times are stratified, so the 240 between them spread their jumps evenly and the mean follows the master equation to within about a degree); their average Bloch vector r̄ is the density matrix, ρ = ½(1 + r̄·σ), with |r̄| = 1 while they agree and less once decoherence has thrown some off (a mixed state). A ring is a test for its target state |t⟩: each run passes with probability |⟨t|ψ⟩|², so the share that pass is the fidelity F̄ = ⟨t|ρ|t⟩ = (1 + r̄·t̂)/2. That is linear in r̄, so aiming the mean is the best any single pulse can do, and it is capped at (1 + |r̄|)/2: once the runs spread, no aim gets most of them through. A ring counts at F̄ ≥ 70 %. Measurement gates read every run and then reset it to |0⟩ (a π pulse conditioned on its own outcome, as labs do); green reset curtains start a fresh shot. Noise clouds measure the runs that touch them.

Left out, on purpose: detuning, so nothing precesses; pure dephasing (noise that scrambles a run's phase without taking its energy), so the only decay is T₁; heating out of |0⟩, since the fridge is taken as cold; and readout imperfections: every measurement here is instantaneous and never wrong. The Virtual Qubit Lab adds detuning, decay during the pulses and the shot noise of a finite number of measurements.
