🎯 Readout Lab

You cannot look at a qubit directly. Instead, it shifts the pitch of a microwave resonator next to it, by −χ when it is in |0⟩ and +χ in |1⟩ (this lab draws χ as a positive number; for the usual transmon sitting below its resonator χ is negative, so on a real chip |1⟩ is the lower peak and the two sides swap). Send in a tone, listen to what comes back, and decide: 0 or 1? Each measurement lands as a dot in the IQ plane; the two clouds of dots must not overlap. Real units throughout: MHz, µs and photons, so a published qubit from Q-Vault loads exactly.

What to look for

    How dispersive readout works

    The idea

    A qubit and a resonator that are far apart in frequency do not swap energy, but they still push on each other. The qubit's state shifts the resonator's frequency by ±χ. Shine a tone at the resonator and the light that leaks back out carries a different amplitude and phase for |0⟩ and |1⟩. A single tone holds only a few photons, so each answer is noisy: the dots form two fuzzy clouds, and the readout is good when the clouds are far apart compared with their size.

    The equations this page solves

    The field α in the resonator, driven at ωd with strength ε, for the qubit in state s (Δs = ωr ∓ χ − ωd):

    dα/dt = −(κ/2 + iΔs) α − iε  →  αs = −iε / (κ/2 + iΔs)

    It rings up to the pointer state αs in a time of about 2/κ. The measured record is the field averaged over τ, plus vacuum noise of variance 1/(4ηκτ) in each quadrature. In steady state, the signal-to-noise ratio and the error of each state are

    SNR = 2√(ηκτ) |α1 − α0|,   P(error) = ½ erfc(SNR / 2√2)

    So SNR grows like √(n̄ τ): four times the photons or four times the listening doubles it. For a fixed photon number the separation is largest when κ = 2χ.

    Why not just listen longer, or louder?

    • Longer: a qubit in |1⟩ can decay during the measurement and its dots slide toward the |0⟩ cloud. With a finite T₁ there is a best τ (the lower right plot).
    • Louder: the dispersive picture holds only below the critical photon number ncrit = (Δ/2g)². Above it, strong drives cause transitions out of the qubit's states.
    • Faster leak (bigger κ): photons leave faster, so the field rings up sooner, but the resonator also drains the qubit through the Purcell effect, TPurcell = (Δ/g)²/κ, unless a Purcell filter blocks it.
    • Dephasing: the photons that tell you the answer also destroy the qubit's phase, at Γd = κ|α0 − α1|²/2. A measurement cannot be gentle and informative at once.
    What this model leaves out
    • The record is a plain average over τ (a box filter). Labs weight the record with a matched filter, which helps when T₁ matters.
    • No Purcell decay, resonator nonlinearity (Kerr), amplifier saturation, or measurement-induced transitions above ncrit (only a warning).
    • Two levels only: real transmons also have |2⟩, which can leak in, or be used on purpose ("shelving") to make |1⟩ easier to tell apart.
    • No heating during the readout, and no internal loss in the resonator (all photons reach the amplifier).
