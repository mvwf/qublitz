Simulation convention

H(t) = 2π·ωqσz/2 + 2π·ΩR[I(t)σx + Q(t)σy]·cos(2π·ωdt), with no rotating-wave approximation. Energy relaxation at rate 1/T₁. Mystery and assigned qubits have T₂ = 2T₁ (no pure dephasing), the teaching model of the ENGS 53 lab; in free play you can lower T₂ to add pure dephasing. Frequencies are in GHz (ω/2π) and times in ns. Results match the Python/qutip implementation to better than 10⁻⁶.

You cannot dial the qubit frequency ωq directly. It is a fixed, hidden property of this qubit. Sweep the drive frequency ωd and read ωq off the resonance, where P(|1⟩) peaks at ωd = ωq.

Use a π/2 – τ – π/2 sequence to build a Ramsey chevron in measured P(|1⟩). For each point the simulator drives at ωref + Δ: it plays a square pulse of your π/2 duration, lets the qubit idle for τ (precessing at its true detuning, with T₁ and T₂ decay), plays the second pulse and measures. So the chevron is an experiment, not a picture: if your reference frequency is off, the chevron's center moves away from Δ = 0 by exactly that error, and if your π/2 pulse is miscalibrated the fringes lose contrast.
