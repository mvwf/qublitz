Simulation convention

H(t) = 2π·ωqσz/2 + 2π·ΩR[I(t)σx + Q(t)σy]·cos(2π·ωdt), written in the engine's basis, where the ground state |0⟩ has σz = −1 (the plots below use the textbook sphere, with |0⟩ at ⟨σz⟩ = +1), and with no rotating-wave approximation. Energy relaxation at rate 1/T₁. Mystery and assigned qubits have T₂ = 2T₁ (no pure dephasing), the teaching model of the ENGS 53 lab; in free play you can lower T₂ to add pure dephasing. Frequencies are in GHz (ω/2π) and times in ns. Results match the Python/qutip implementation to better than 10⁻⁶.

🌱 New here? What this lab is

A qubit here is a tiny electrical circuit, cooled to a hundredth of a degree above absolute zero, that can be in a mix of two states, |0⟩ and |1⟩. You never see the mix directly: you nudge the qubit with a microwave pulse, then measure it many times and count how often it shows 1. Everything you learn comes from that count, P(|1⟩).

  1. Find its note. Every qubit answers to one frequency, like a tuning fork. On Frequency Domain, press Run frequency sweep and look for the bright stripe.
  2. Flip it. Drive it at that frequency for just the right time and it turns from |0⟩ to |1⟩: a π pulse.
  3. Watch it forget. Left alone, a flipped qubit slowly falls back to |0⟩. How long it lasts, T₁, is what engineers fight for.

Easier first steps: fly a qubit around the sphere in 🚁 Qubit Copter, or race the decay in 🌀 Rabi Rider. Ready for the equations? Set the depth to 🎓 Student in the site bar.

🔬 Questions this model can answer, and ones it cannot

  • Measure the Bloch–Siegert shift. The lab keeps the counter-rotating terms. Sweep a narrow window at two Rabi rates in free play and check that the peak moves by roughly ΩR²/(4ωq) (Ω and ω as frequencies): about 16 MHz at 500 MHz and 4 GHz. Which way does it move, and does the answer depend on exactly when the pulse ends relative to the drive's oscillation?
  • T₂* or T₂? The model has no slow noise, so a Ramsey envelope gives T₂ itself. Which experiment would separate T₂* from T₂ on a real qubit, and what would its envelope look like with 1/f flux noise?
  • What a two-level model hides. No |2⟩ level, so no leakage and no need for DRAG pulses. Look up a real transmon's EJ/EC and anharmonicity in Q-Vault Levels and estimate the shortest safe π pulse.
  • Your own fits. Download the CSVs and fit them yourself in Python: do your error bars agree with the page's?

Lab checklist for this qubit

  1. Find the resonance. Run a frequency sweep whose peak lands inside the window.
  2. Flip the qubit. On the Time Domain tab, drive at the sweep peak with a pulse that leaves P(|1⟩) above 0.9 when it ends.
  3. Measure T₁. After that flip, get a T₁ fit within 20% of the true value. Leave plenty of idle time after the pulse.
  4. Pin ωq with Ramsey. Fit a chevron cut so that one of its two ωq candidates is within 1 MHz of the true value.

Use a π/2 – τ – π/2 sequence to build a Ramsey chevron in measured P(|1⟩). For each point the simulator drives at ωref + Δ: it plays a square pulse of your π/2 duration, lets the qubit idle for τ (precessing at its true detuning, with T₁ and T₂ decay), plays the second pulse and measures. So the chevron is an experiment, not a picture: if your reference frequency is off, the chevron's center moves away from Δ = 0 by exactly that error, and if your π/2 pulse is miscalibrated the fringes lose contrast.
