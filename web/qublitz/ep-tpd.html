🎯 Exceptional Points & Transmission Peak Degeneracies

Explore where exceptional points (EPs) and transmission-peak degeneracies (TPDs) coincide in a tunable magnon–photon dimer. Based on A. S. Carney, J. S. Salcedo-Gallo, S. K. Bedkihal & M. Fitzpatrick, The Role of Exceptional Points and Transmission Peak Degeneracies in Non-Hermitian Sensing, Phys. Rev. Applied 25, 064031 (2026).

Challenges

  1. Which marker moves? Keep φ fixed and change the cavity damping κ̃c by at least 1. Watch the red × (EP) and the red ○ (TPD).
  2. Line them up. Find the coupling phase φ that puts the primary EP and the primary TPD both on the Δ̃f axis (Δ̃κ = 0).
  3. Make them meet. Bring the primary TPD within 0.05 of the primary EP (the distance is under the table on the right).

Each challenge ticks itself when the sliders meet its goal.

Dynamical matrix of the dimer

Ã = −i(f̃c − f̃d) − κ̃c2 −i −i eiφ −i(f̃c − f̃d − Δ̃f) + (Δ̃κ − κ̃c2)

Frequencies and damping rates are in units of the coupling J. The map below is drawn over the detuning Δ̃f and the damping asymmetry Δ̃κ, which is half the difference of the two damping rates (so the exceptional point at φ = 0 sits where the damping rates differ by 4J).

Petermann factor over (Δ̃κ, Δ̃f)

Color shows the Petermann factor where the system is stable (instability ≤ 0); the gray region is unstable. Dashed lines mark where q̃ = 0, the discriminant = 0, the instability boundary, and where the Petermann factor reaches its minimum of 1. Red marks are the primary EP (×) and TPD (○); gray marks are the secondary and “rogue” ones.

Primary EP and TPD peak splitting

Along a cut through the primary EP: the transmission peaks ν± (purple) merge at the TPD, while the eigenfrequencies −Im λ± (dashed) merge at the EP.

Relevant equations

Eigenvalues of the dynamical matrix:

λ̃0 = (Δ̃κ2 − κ̃c2) + i(Δ̃f2 − f̃c + f̃d)
Δ̃λ = √−Δ̃f2 + 2i Δ̃fΔ̃κ + Δ̃κ2 − 4eiφ
λ̃± = λ̃0 ± Δ̃λ2

Petermann factor:

K̄2 = Δ̃f2 + Δ̃κ2 + |Δ̃λ|2 + 42|Δ̃λ|2

TPD equations:

0 = f3 + p̃ f + q̃
p̃ = (κ̃c − Δ̃κ)2 + Re(Δ̃λ2)4,  q̃ = (κ̃c − Δ̃κ) Im(Δ̃λ2)8

Carney, A. S., Salcedo-Gallo, J. S., Bedkihal, S. K., & Fitzpatrick, M. (2026). The Role of Exceptional Points and Transmission Peak Degeneracies in Non-Hermitian Sensing. Physical Review Applied, 25, 064031. doi:10.1103/mfq8-gzsj (preprint: arXiv:2506.09141).
