❄️ Dilution Refrigerator Noise Explorer

A drive line carries room-temperature thermal noise down into the fridge. Each attenuator absorbs part of the noise coming in and adds back its own thermal noise at that stage's temperature. Change the stage temperatures and attenuations below to see how much thermal noise reaches the mixing chamber (MXC), where the qubit sits.

Challenges

  1. See the problem. Set every attenuator to 0 dB and read neff at the MXC.
  2. Waste 60 dB. Use at least 60 dB in total but still leave more than 10 thermal photons at the MXC.
  3. Spend it well. With at most 60 dB in total and at most 30 dB on the MXC (its cooling power is small), get below 0.01 thermal photons at the MXC.
  4. Warm the MXC. Keep an attenuator on the MXC and raise its temperature to 100 mK or more. Which noise takes over?

Challenges are judged at 5 GHz with the other plates at their standard temperatures (Reset restores them).

Math used in this page

For a mode at frequency f and temperature T, the mean thermal photon number is

n̄(T, f) = 1exp(hf / kBT) − 1

Each stage has a physical temperature Ti and an attenuation Ai in dB, which is linear attenuation Li = 10Ai/10. The attenuator is a thermal beam splitter at Ti: it passes 1/Li of the incoming noise n̄in and adds its own thermal noise for the rest,

n̄out = n̄inLi + (1 − 1Li) n̄(Ti, f)

The output of stage i is the input to stage i+1, and the line starts with thermal noise at the top stage's temperature. The effective temperature Teff is the temperature whose thermal occupation equals n̄eff:

n̄eff(f) = 1exp(hf / kBTeff) − 1

and the noise reduction versus room temperature is 10 log10(n̄room(f) / n̄eff(f)).

What the qubit feels. If the thermal photons on the line at the qubit's frequency are what set its temperature, the qubit equilibrates to the line: Teff = hf / (kB ln(1 + 1/n̄)), and a two-level system at that temperature has excited-state population

Pexc = 1exp(hf / kBTeff) + 1 = n̄1 + 2n̄,  PexcPground = e−hf/kBTeff = n̄1 + n̄

(the ratio is also the ratio of the photon-driven up and down rates, n̄γ and (n̄ + 1)γ). On the readout line, the same n̄ photons sit in the readout resonator (linewidth κ, dispersive shift ±χ) and each one that passes through carries away which-state information, so the qubit dephases at

Γφ = n̄ κ (2χ)²κ² + (2χ)² (n̄ ≪ 1),  Tφ = 1/Γφ

with κ and χ as angular rates (2π × the values in MHz). This is the small-n̄ limit of A. A. Clerk and D. W. Utami, Phys. Rev. A 75, 042302 (2007), Γφ = (κ/2) Re[√((1 + 2iχ/κ)² + 8iχn̄/κ) − 1], in the form used by F. Yan et al., Nat. Commun. 7, 12964 (2016). For χ ≫ κ every photon dephases, Γφ → n̄κ; for χ ≪ κ, Γφ → 4χ²n̄/κ. The page quotes the full expression when n̄ is not small. The drive line and the readout line are two different cables; the panel applies this page's one chain to both roles in turn.

The drive power at each stage is the generator power minus the attenuation so far, and the cable insertion loss is taken off at the MXC. In this model the cable loss is applied only to the drive, and attenuation does not depend on frequency. A real lossy cable also attenuates the noise and re-emits at its own temperature, so the noise shown is a slight overestimate. The drive photon flux is P/hf.
