❄️ Dilution Refrigerator Noise Explorer

A drive line carries room-temperature thermal noise down into the fridge. Each attenuator absorbs part of the noise coming in and adds back its own thermal noise at that stage's temperature. Change the stage temperatures and attenuations below to see how much thermal noise reaches the mixing chamber (MXC), where the qubit sits.

Math used in this page

For a mode at frequency f and temperature T, the mean thermal photon number is

n̄(T, f) = 1exp(hf / kBT) − 1

Each stage has a physical temperature Ti and an attenuation Ai in dB, which is linear attenuation Li = 10Ai/10. The attenuator is a thermal beam splitter at Ti: it passes 1/Li of the incoming noise n̄in and adds its own thermal noise for the rest,

n̄out = n̄inLi + (1 − 1Li) n̄(Ti, f)

The output of stage i is the input to stage i+1, and the line starts with thermal noise at the top stage's temperature. The effective temperature Teff is the temperature whose thermal occupation equals n̄eff:

n̄eff(f) = 1exp(hf / kBTeff) − 1

and the noise reduction versus room temperature is 10 log10(n̄room(f) / n̄eff(f)).

The drive power at each stage is the generator power minus the attenuation so far, and the cable insertion loss is taken off at the MXC. The cable loss only affects the drive, not the noise, and attenuation does not depend on frequency. The drive photon flux is P/hf.
