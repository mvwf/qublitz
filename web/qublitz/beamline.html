🔦 Beamline route a laser pulse to set each qubit

A puzzle about pulses. The laser is a resonant drive: a pulse turns every qubit it passes through, about an axis set by its drive phase and by an angle equal to its pulse area. Place mirrors to route it, phase plates to change the axis and attenuators to change the angle, then fire and bring every qubit to its gold target. Nothing turns unless the beam crosses it.

The physics

Seen from a frame that rotates with the drive, a qubit driven exactly on resonance feels only the drive: there is no free precession, so a qubit's state changes only while a pulse is passing through it. A pulse with drive phase φ and Rabi rate Ω, on for a time t, turns the Bloch vector about the equatorial axis (cos φ, sin φ, 0) by its area:

H = (ħΩ/2)(cos φ σx + sin φ σy),    θ = ∫Ω dt,    P(1) = sin²(θ/2) starting from |0⟩
  • Mirror: changes where the pulse goes, not its area. In a chip the drive arrives down a wire; what reaches the qubit is its phase and area.
  • Phase plate: shifts the drive phase by a quarter period, φ → φ + 90° (which sign a delay gives is a convention; the lab adds 90°). That turns an x rotation into a y rotation; two plates reverse the sense of the turn.
  • Attenuator: −6 dB halves the field amplitude Ω. The pulse is just as long, so its area θ = Ω·t halves: π → π/2 → π/4.
  • Order: lasers fire one after another (1 then 2), and a beam that crosses a qubit twice turns it twice, in the order it arrives. Turns about different axes do not commute.

What the game leaves out. The beam keeps its full strength along the board (each qubit absorbs a negligible share), pulses are short enough that nothing decays while they fly (no T₁ or T₂ here), and there is no beam splitter: a passive 50/50 splitter would send θ/√2 down each branch. A real route also shifts the drive phase: every wavelength of path length and every mirror bounce adds carrier phase. The game takes those as calibrated out (as an experiment does by tuning each line's delay), so only the phase plates change φ. A qubit's global phase is not shown: after a 2π turn the state is −|0⟩, which no measurement of that qubit alone can tell from |0⟩.
