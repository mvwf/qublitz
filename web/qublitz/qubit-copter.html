🚁 Qubit Copter steer a qubit through its projections (prototype)

The drive is on resonance, so nothing moves unless you drive it. The big sphere shows your qubit; the three strips scroll in time like a helicopter game and show its projections ⟨σx⟩, ⟨σy⟩ and ⟨σz⟩, which are what repeated measurements along x, y and z would average to. Each target is a window on all three strips at once, and a ring on the sphere: be in all three windows when it arrives. A companion to Rabi Rider.

Reading the strips

Time runs right to left; the copter is now. Gold windows are targets (be inside all three), red blocks are walls. Since x² + y² + z² = 1, the three windows together pin down one state.

The physics

In the frame rotating with a drive that is exactly on resonance, the qubit's Hamiltonian has no σz term:

H = (ħΩ/2)(ax σx + ay σy),  dr/dt = ω × r,  ω = Ω (ax, ay, 0)

so the Bloch vector r = (⟨σx⟩, ⟨σy⟩, ⟨σz⟩) stays put unless driven, and a drive with phase α turns it about the equatorial axis (cos α, sin α, 0) at the Rabi rate Ω (here 1.8 rad/s, so a π pulse takes 1.75 s). The amplitude ramps with a 0.12 s time constant.

The three strips are the projections ⟨σx⟩, ⟨σy⟩ and ⟨σz⟩: what you would get by averaging many measurements along each axis (state tomography). Because a pure state has x² + y² + z² = 1, fixing all three fixes the state, which is why a target is three windows at once. A wall that forbids, say, ⟨σz⟩ < −0.4 cuts the cap around |1⟩ off the sphere.

Point to steer uses a neat fact: a rotation about an equatorial axis n keeps r·n fixed, so it joins r to a point p in one turn when (r − p)·n = 0, that is n ∥ (r − p) × z. That is a real choice of drive phase, not a cheat. Circuit targets are the states a random gate sequence (H, S, T, X, Y, Z, √X, √Y, Rx(π/4), Ry(π/3)) makes from |0⟩; the dotted arc is the path the gate's own rotation would take, which is often not the path your drive takes.
